Intro
Are statewide economic trends associated with changes in Charlotte’s attendance and absence rates from 2019–2025? <Bold This>
In this research project I pulled several sources of data using an API from United States Census Bureau (census.gov) and Charlotte Quality of Life Explorer to explore on how High School attendance has been affected ever since covid-19.
Ever since Covid-19 attendance records have never recovered and are not improving. Thomas S. Dee defines absenteeism as "Chronic absenteeism, defined as missing 10 percent or more of school for any reason" (pnas.org). There are several variables as to why this can be. Morgan Polikoff in her article explains that some of the factors are students with disabilities, homelessness, but with it's highest metric being low-income students in which my data provides a little more insight on to that.

Data
I pulled several data points using an API from United States Census Bureau for North Carolina specifically. Highlighting several economic factors since Covid-19. This data revealed a drastic rise faster than it did in pre-Covid-19 in Median Household income, Median homeowner cost to simply own a home, and a rise in employment for teenagers ages 16-19. To add to my data sources, I pulled student absence data from the Charlotte Quality of Life Explorer. Using these two sources I combined and cleaned the data into one data set. Below I have created two graphs to help make this data as transparent as possible. 

Conclusions
The data collected revealed several factors that could explain chronic absenteeism for high schoolers. The first graph here simply shows us that high school attendance dipped heavily with Covid-19 and never recovered, as well showing that student enrollment is not factor as it stays consistent. The first graph here shows three rising factors that could play into attendance. The first being median household income. While house hold income has risen, it does not account for inflation. To help account and show inflation that we pull median owner cost which shows us rising cost to own and operate a home, which is rising at a similar rate to income. With that lastly pull the variable for teenager employment from ages 16-19 since 2019. A Journal of Research on Adolescence explains to us that it has always been a historic trend that increasing work productivity can have a negative impact on school and attendance. The graph  These higher factors add stress to families and students attending high school for the first time. Adding stress to house income and homeowner cost not only reduces budgets but can also cause displacement for those families increasing absences and forcing teenagers to work.
