Intro
Are statewide economic trends associated with changes in Charlotte’s attendance and absence rates from 2019–2025? <Bold This>
In this research project I pulled several sources of data using an API from United States Census Bureau (census.gov) and Charlotte Quality of Life Explorer to explore on how High School attendance has been affected ever since covid-19.
Ever since Covid-19 attendance records have never recovered and are not improving. Thomas S. Dee defines absenteeism as "Chronic absenteeism, defined as missing 10 percent or more of school for any reason" (pnas.org). There are several variables as to why this can be.
Morgan Polikoff in her article explains that some of the factors are students with disabilities, homelessness, but with it's highest metric being low-income students in which my data provides a little more insight on to that.

Data
I pulled several data points from United States Census Bureau for North Carolina specifically. Highlighting several economic factors since Covid-19. This data provided insight into Median homeowner cost to own a home, Median household income, and employment trends for teenagers ages 16-19. To add to my data sources, I pulled student absence data from the Charlotte Quality of Life Explorer. Using these two sources I combined and cleaned the data into one data set. Below I have created two graphs to help make this data as transparent as possible. 

Conclusions
The data collected revealed several things to us. As a base line, student attendance was shown not to be a result of enrollment. Enrollment over the past few years has stayed consistent before and after Covid-19. From the United States Census Bureau ACS Report, showing rising homeowner cost and reduced HouseholdThe These higher factors add stress to families and students attending high school for the first time. Adding stress to house income and homeowner cost not only reduces budgets but can also cause displacement for those families increasing absences and forcing teenagers to work.
